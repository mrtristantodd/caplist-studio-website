# Caplist Studio homepage concept comparison

The buyer-first copy rewrite changes the job of the homepage from explaining Caplist as a media-production system to making the commercial opportunity immediately obvious to a first-time property-media visitor.

To test the right presentation, two homepage concepts are being built with the same core message and the same Caplist visual identity.

## What stays identical

- Core proposition: **Capture once. Sell more.**
- Audience: professional real-estate photographers, videographers and property-media businesses.
- Commercial story: completed property media can become additional finished products the same client can buy.
- Effort story: no second property visit and no new full manual editing queue.
- Operating model: Caplist handles production; the media business chooses what to offer, packages it and sets the client price.
- Quality position: professional input, quality gating and property fidelity.
- Current launch product set only: Photo Reel, Vertical Video Reel, Property Teaser, Feature Reel and Mixed Media.
- Interactive Floorplan is deliberately excluded until a working prototype exists.

## Concept A — Editorial / text-led

The restrained version keeps motion secondary and relies on hierarchy, typography, property imagery and a static transformation board.

### Hero presentation

**One completed property shoot**

27 photos + property video + drone

→ Caplist Studio

→ Photo Reel / Vertical Video Reel / Property Teaser / Feature Reel / Mixed Media

**One completed job can become a bigger product menu.**

### Main explanatory device

A four-step static sequence:

1. Upload the completed job.
2. See what else you could sell.
3. Choose the product.
4. Sell it through your business.

### Intended perception

Premium, calm, credible, editorial and easy to scan. The test is whether excellent copy and visual hierarchy explain the product without needing a product animation.

---

## Concept B — Animated product walkthrough

The animated version uses the same commercial story but demonstrates the workflow directly.

### Hero presentation

A restrained mini-animation cycles through:

1. **Media uploaded** — 27 photos, property video and drone.
2. **Caplist checking the media**.
3. **5 products available**.

### Main walkthrough

A larger Caplist-style interface demonstrates:

1. Completed property shoot uploaded.
2. Caplist checks media type, quality and suitability.
3. Five additional product opportunities appear.
4. Vertical Video Reel is selected.
5. The user makes three focused choices: style, duration and music direction.
6. The finished reel reaches **Ready**.
7. Final message: **Same shoot. Another finished product to sell.**

The walkthrough is deliberately a controlled marketing demonstration, not a recording of a live backend. This allows the concept to stay visually accurate, lightweight, responsive and easy to update as the real application evolves.

### Intended perception

Immediate comprehension, visible simplicity and stronger product proof without presenting Caplist as an AI novelty tool.

## Design changes shared by both concepts

| Current presentation | Proposed presentation |
|---|---|
| Hero asks the visitor to interpret a source-to-product diagram | Hero directly links one completed shoot to additional products and revenue opportunity |
| Product sections explain capabilities at similar visual weight | Commercial outcome comes first; source requirements are secondary |
| Product Explorer feels like an eligibility engine | Product story is framed around **what else could you sell?** |
| Six equal business-benefit cards | Three primary outcomes: make each booking worth more, avoid another editing queue, keep the client relationship |
| Workflow is mainly process reassurance | Workflow visually shows where Caplist slots in after the primary edit |
| Quality is a general product principle | Quality is tied directly to the photographer’s reputation: good enough to carry your name |
| Long editorial sections repeat similar structures | More deliberate rhythm: promise → proof → explanation → outcome |

## Comparison criteria

Both concepts should be judged against the same questions:

1. Can a first-time visitor tell what Caplist is within five seconds?
2. Do they understand what it does with media they already capture?
3. Do they understand why that matters commercially?
4. Does the process feel simple enough to try?
5. Can they picture the finished products they would sell?
6. Does it feel credible and professional rather than gimmicky?
7. Does it make them want to see more or book a demo?

The preferred concept should win on comprehension and commercial clarity, not simply visual novelty.
